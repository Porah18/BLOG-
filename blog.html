It is Time Ghanaian Movies Evolved __ The Missing Ingredient in Ghanaian Movies.!

I’ve been thinking about the Ghanaian film industry, and honestly, I believe I know why it feels stuck. It’s not that we don’t have talent — it’s that we keep telling the same stories the same way. Most Ghanaian movies are dominated by adults, with very little involvement from young people or children. The youth are barely represented, yet we are the ones watching, streaming, and shaping trends today. That disconnect matters. Another issue is the storytelling. Almost every movie revolves around witchcraft, spiritual attacks, or the same old fear-based themes. Yes, those stories exist — but is that all Ghanaian life is about? What about school life, friendships, church programs, youth struggles, family bonds, and everyday experiences we can actually relate to? It feels like little effort is put into creativity and modern storytelling. If the industry truly wants to grow, it needs to involve young people, diversify its stories, and reflect real Ghanaian life today — not just recycle the past. Until then, Ghanaian movies will continue to feel outdated and disconnected from the audience they need the most.


A Simple Childhood Moment That Made Science Make Sense.

We learn about the Earth in school — how it rotates, revolves, and is spherical — yet many people still don’t fully understand it or even believe it. For a long time, those ideas felt abstract to me too. But then I remembered something from childhood. As children, we often felt like the sun or the moon was following us. You’d be running around the neighbourhood, the sun hitting you hard, and no matter where you moved, it still felt like it was right there — chasing you. We would laugh and complain, saying, “The sun is following me!” Now I realise something important: the sun was never following us. We were the ones moving — on a spherical Earth, rotating and revolving. That simple childhood experience suddenly made everything click for me. The movement, the light, the angle — it all made sense. That’s when I truly understood that the Earth is not flat or imaginary in shape. It is spherical, and we are constantly moving on it, even when it feels like we’re standing still. Sometimes, understanding science doesn’t come from formulas — it comes from lived experience.
